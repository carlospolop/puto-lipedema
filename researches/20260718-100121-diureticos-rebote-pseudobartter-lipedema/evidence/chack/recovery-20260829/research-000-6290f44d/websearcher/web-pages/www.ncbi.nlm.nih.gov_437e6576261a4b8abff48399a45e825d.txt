Therefore, patient access to appropriate and timely treatment is often diminished   and patients frequently find themselves blamed for their condition, including self-blame. In addition to adipocytes, immune cells and fibroblasts, LCT has an extracellular matrix of fibers (e.g. collagen and elastin) that supports, protects, and connects tissues. Blood vessels and cells contribute fluid to the extracellular matrix. Fluid exits through lymphatic vessels   or remains in the tissue bound to glycosaminoglycans and proteoglycans. Glycosaminoglycans increase when extracellular matrix water and/or salt increases.</text></passage><passage><infon key="section_type">METHODS</infon><infon key="type">paragraph</infon><offset>7749</offset><text>When excess fluid is present, LCT becomes compliant,   allowing more fluid to collect, stimulating proteoglycan synthesis. When excess fluid collects in the extracellular matrix, it is called edema.  </text></passage><passage><infon key="section_type">METHODS</infon><infon key="type">title_2</infon><offset>8134</offset><text>1.2 Extracellular matrix edema in lipedema tissue is bound to proteoglycans.
